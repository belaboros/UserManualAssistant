# Tesla Launches Article 39 Evidence Dashboard
2026-09-01
Government Post

Tesla has published a new "Article 39 Evidence Dashboard" a comprehensive safety data package aimed directly at European regulators. The dashboard is built to support Tesla's exemption requests from UN Regulation No. 171.01 (DCAS), the rule set currently standing between FSD (Supervised) and a full EU rollout. It compiles eight studies covering fleet mileage, collision rates, AEB activations, lane-change safety, driver attentiveness, and more — spanning both the North American customer fleet and Tesla's European engineering fleet. The European-specific evidence is the real headline: Tesla reports over 793,000 engineering fleet miles across 8 European countries with zero major or minor collisions attributed to FSD, plus 230,000+ structured scenario tests across six European cities (roundabouts, intersections, crosswalks, cyclist yields) with a pass rate above 99%. Combined with data showing 40-69% lower collision rates and up to 97% fewer AEB activations versus manually driven Tesla vehicles in some categories, this is by far the most detailed public evidence package Tesla has released in support of its push for regulatory approval in Europe.

# Switzerland — FSD ride-alongs launched
2026-08-11

Tesla kicked off FSD (Supervised) ride-along events in Switzerland on August 11. Sessions are running in Cham, Bern, Buchs-Aarau, Zürich, Bussigny, Basel, and Genève, with the Zürich location booked through September 30. As in other European markets, customers ride in the passenger seat while a Tesla employee stays behind the wheel — this is a demo program, not an approval for private use. The rides operate under a trial permit from Switzerland's Federal Roads Office (ASTRA), which classifies FSD Supervised as a Level 2+ system requiring full driver attention at all times (Article 31 SVG). There's currently no formal application for general approval in Switzerland.

# Summary Record from the 05 May 2026 TCMV Meeting
2026-08-18

The summary record from the 05 May 2026 TCMV meeting is now out. The Netherlands gave an update on the Article 39 provisional approval for Tesla FSD, walking through its features, capabilities, and exemptions under UNR171, and pushed for a decision by 30 June — though the EC said that timeline isn't realistic given the data analysis still needed. The "speed offset" feature drew pushback from two Member States, who worry it could enable over-speeding, while NL argued it can actually help by correcting wrong speed limits and adapting to traffic flow. One MS stressed that making the exemptions permanent will require the EC to propose actual amendments to UNR171 at UNECE/GRVA, with no guarantee of acceptance. NL also confirmed a long-term effects study is underway and that consolidated Q&As will land on Circabc, while another MS called for a harmonized EU communication and raised questions about how FSD interacts with ADDW. The EC has asked NL to present again at the upcoming MVWG and ACV sub-group meetings for transparency.


# France Declines to Approve FSD, Cites Speeding and Attention Gaps
2026-07-21

French Transport Minister Philippe Tabarot issued a video statement rejecting Tesla's push for national FSD (Supervised) approval, stating the safety trade-offs are "not yet sufficient to authorize it as it currently stands." He cited two concrete concerns: the system permitting speeds above posted limits when surrounding traffic moves faster, and insufficient driver-attention monitoring during complex urban maneuvers like lane changes, intersections, and roundabouts. Electrek Notably, this isn't an outright rejection — France is declining to unilaterally mirror the Netherlands' national approval and instead wants an EU-wide sign-off, with Tabarot noting continued technical talks with the Netherlands, Tesla, and other member states, plus a French autonomous-vehicle industry summit planned for autumn. Elon Musk responded on X that delaying FSD approval "will cost lives". France becomes the first major EU economy to detail its objections at cabinet level, following months of deferral to the European Commission's Article 39 review process.

# Norway Pushes Back as Netherlands Seeks EU-Wide Exemption for Tesla FSD
2026-07-07

Norway's road authority, Statens Vegvesen, has raised several concerns about the Netherlands' (RDW) push for an EU-wide Article 39 exemption for Tesla FSD (Supervised) under UN Regulation 171. Norway argues that key elements of RDW's approval — allowing activation beyond motorways, and exempting the system from strict speed-limit compliance and lateral-acceleration limits in curves — depart from the stepwise rollout approach and safety provisions Norway helped negotiate into the regulation. Norway also notes that RDW's own safety assessment rates the net impact as only "marginally positive," and states that Tesla's public marketing does not fully align with the technical documentation submitted to regulators. As an EEA member, Norway has no voting rights in the EU's Technical Committee – Motor Vehicles (TCMV), where the exemption is being decided, but has submitted detailed written questions ahead of committee sessions. Separately, Norway rejected Tesla's proposed customer-demonstration materials for FSD in May 2026, citing insufficient disclosure that the system is a driver assistance feature rather than autonomous driving; as of early July, Tesla has not submitted revised materials. The next opportunity for a formal EU-wide vote is expected at the October 2026 TCMV meeting.

# FSD Supervised Approved in the Netherlands — First EU Type Approval Issued
2026-04-10

RDW has issued a European type approval for Tesla's Full Self-Driving (Supervised) driver assistance system, with provisional validity in the Netherlands. The system was extensively examined and tested for more than one and a half years on test tracks and public roads. RDW emphasizes that FSD Supervised is NOT autonomous or self-driving — the driver remains responsible and must always remain in control. The approval is currently valid only in the Netherlands, but RDW will submit the application for EU-wide permission to the European Commission, where all member states will vote. If approved by majority, the system may be used across all EU member states. RDW notes that the EU version of FSD Supervised is not comparable to the US version, as Europe imposes different and stricter requirements on safety.
